    The two research threads¶

    Both threads are candidate answers to my original question. They are not competitors to pick between yet — the goal for these three days is to understand both well enough to discuss them, and to see how RobotArena∞ could test either.

    Thread A — "Embodiment tax": does action fine-tuning erase visual understanding?¶

    This is now a named, active research area, directly matching my question to Prof. Shi.

    • Actions as Language / VLM2VLA (arXiv 2509.22195) — diagnosis: catastrophic forgetting comes from a distribution mismatch between the VLM's Internet-scale pretraining corpus and narrow robot fine-tuning data. Fix: represent actions as language so LoRA fine-tuning stays close to the pretrained distribution.
    • UAM: A Dual-Stream Perspective on Forgetting in VLA Training (arXiv 2605.15735) — diagnosis: the problem is architectural, not just data distribution. A single encoder is forced to serve both language-grounded semantics and control-relevant visual features. Fix: a second "dorsal" pathway dedicated to visuomotor/dynamics prediction, inspired by the two-stream (ventral/dorsal) organization of biological vision.
    • TwinBrainVLA (arXiv 2601.14133) — same diagnosis as UAM (semantic pretraining objective vs. spatially-grounded control objective are fundamentally in tension), different architecture (asymmetric mixture-of-transformers).

    Key tension to resolve while reading: is the cause (1) data distribution mismatch (fixable with LoRA/data reformulation, per VLM2VLA) or (2) a structural bottleneck in sharing one encoder for two jobs (per UAM/TwinBrainVLA)? These predict different experiments and different fixes.

    Thread B — Video-based action models (the Simchowitz–Shi direction)¶

    • Video Prediction Policy (VPP) — hypothesis: video diffusion models (VDMs) implicitly encode predictive visual representations (current state + future dynamics), unlike single-image encoders that only capture static information. VPP conditions an inverse-dynamics action head on these predictive representations.
    • Turning Video Models into Generalist Robot Policies (VERA) (arXiv 2605.27817) — deliberately decouples an embodiment-agnostic video planner from an embodiment-specific inverse-dynamics model (a Jacobian-based IDM), rather than fusing video and action into one model. Directly relevant to cross-embodiment and real-to-sim thinking.
    • Motus (CVPR 2026) — the opposite architectural bet: fuse understanding, video generation, and action into one Mixture-of-Transformers model with a shared latent-action space. Useful as a contrast case to VERA's decoupling.

    Key tension to resolve while reading: should dynamics be obtained by generating video and then inverting it into actions (VPP, VERA), or by training one model to jointly denoise video and action tokens (Motus, and the world-action-model family)? What does each choice cost in data requirements and modularity?

    Why these threads are actually one question¶

    Both threads propose that static-image visual representations are structurally insufficient for dynamic control, and offer two different fixes: separate the pathways (Thread A), or get the missing dynamics signal from video instead of images (Thread B). This synthesis — not either thread alone — is the sharper version of my original question, and it directly reuses RobotArena∞'s finding as motivating evidence: policies conditioned only on static per-frame visual features are exactly the ones I already showed are brittle to scene perturbation.

    Day 2 — Where does generalization actually come from?¶

    Goal: Separate generalization claims that come from data diversity, from pretrained representations, from policy architecture, and from a weak evaluation protocol — a discipline I already partly built into RobotArena∞'s perturbation axes.

    Read, in order:

    1. Video Prediction Policy (VPP) — generalization via predictive visual representations from video diffusion.
    2. VERA (arXiv 2605.27817) — generalization via decoupled video planning + inverse dynamics.
    3. Start Shubham Tulsiani's line: Track2Act or Gen2Act — web/video-derived visual signals for manipulation generalization.

    Day 3 — Reliability, control, and faculty-specific preparation¶

    Goal: Connect imitation-trained VLAs to online adaptation, RL, learned dynamics/control, and safety — and prepare concretely for the Guanya Shi meeting, since that relationship is furthest along.

    Read, in order:

    1. Much Ado About Noising: Dispelling the Myths of Generative Robotic Control (2025/2026, Chaoyi Pan, ..., Guanya Shi, Max Simchowitz — co-authored by both!) — a critical examination of common assumptions in generative robotic control. This is the single most important paper to read closely before the Guanya Shi meeting, since it is co-authored by both faculty I've already spoken with.
    2. Faculty picks for the two I have not yet contacted: - Shubham Tulsiani: Gen2Act (CoRL 2025) and/or DemoDiffusion (ICRA 2026). - Kris Kitani: ExpertAF: Expert Actionable Feedback from Video (CVPR 2025) — deriving actionable coaching feedback from egocentric video, language, and 3D pose; also connects to ASAP's use of human-motion data for sim-to-real humanoid learning.

    Sharpened questions for the Guanya Shi meeting¶

    Replacing my original broad question with three literature-grounded ones:

    1. UAM argues VLA forgetting is architectural (one encoder serving two incompatible jobs), while VLM2VLA argues it is a data-distribution problem fixable with LoRA. What does "Much Ado About Noising" suggest about which failure mode is more fundamental for generative robotic control specifically?
    2. VERA deliberately decouples a video planner from an embodiment-specific inverse-dynamics model. Could RobotArena∞'s real-to-sim pipeline be used to directly measure whether that decoupling improves robustness to the perturbations I already study (texture, object placement), compared to end-to-end VLAs like π₀?
    3. Is the "video-based action models" direction you discussed with Prof. Simchowitz closer to VPP (video model as a representation/conditioning source) or to VERA/Motus (video model inside the closed control loop)? Which is a better fit for agile, physically-grounded control, given your ASAP/AnyCar work?

    π₀.₅ (2025) Co-training with heterogeneous semantic data for new-home/open-world transfer. Which conditioning signals are essential for the claimed generalization?

    Faculty lenses (full reading tracks)¶

    Max Simchowitz — foundations of learning, control, and generative policies¶

    Central question: why do generative robot policies work, when do they fail, and how can they be adapted with fewer expensive robot trials?

    • msimchowitz.github.io — research statement and selected publications.
    • Much Ado About Noising (2025/2026) — co-authored with Guanya Shi; read this closely.
    • Diffusion Policy Policy Optimization (ICLR 2025).
    • The Pitfalls of Imitation Learning when Actions are Continuous (COLT 2025).
    • Background anchor: Learning Without Mixing — system identification from dependent trajectories.

    Guanya Shi — adaptive control, learned dynamics, and safety¶

    Central question: how can learning-based systems remain performant and safe when dynamics, terrain, sensing, or hardware differ from training?

    • ASAP (RSS 2025) — sim-to-real physics grounding for agile humanoid whole-body skills.
    • AnyCar — foundation-style dynamics model + MPC.
    • Agile Mobility with Rapid Online Adaptation — uncertainty-aware adaptation.
    • Q-learning-based Model-free Safety Filter — a safety layer that can filter an arbitrary nominal policy.
    • Much Ado About Noising — shared with Simchowitz.

    Shubham Tulsiani — physical visual perception and generalizable manipulation¶

    Central question: what visual or physical representation lets a robot act correctly in a scene it has not seen before?

    • Track2Act — point tracks from Internet video for zero-shot manipulation.
    • Gen2Act (CoRL 2025) — generated human videos as supervision for manipulation.
    • DemoDiffusion (ICRA 2026) — one-shot human imitation using a pretrained diffusion policy.
    • GHOST (RSS 2026) — hierarchical subgoal policies for generalized manipulation.

    Kris Kitani — egocentric vision, human behavior, and embodied AI¶

    Central question: how can a system infer human intent, skill, and corrective feedback from visual observation, and turn that into useful robot behavior?

    • ExpertAF (CVPR 2025) — actionable coaching feedback generated from video, language, and 3D pose.
    • Research areas to skim: first-person/egocentric vision, human activity forecasting, inverse reinforcement learning, assistive robotics.
    • Natural bridge to Guanya Shi: ASAP and related humanoid work rely on human-motion data for sim-to-real learning — Kitani's egocentric/human-activity expertise is directly relevant to how that data is collected and modeled.

    Note discipline¶

    For every paper, use the existing paper template. Do not write a long chronological summary while reading. Complete only:

    1. the one-sentence claim;
    2. the concrete problem/setup (inputs, outputs, training signal, deployment loop);
    3. one piece of evidence and one limitation;
    4. one question connecting it to another paper or to RobotArena∞;
    5. mark unresolved points explicitly as Open question rather than silently adopting the authors' framing as fact.

    Research statement draft (Day 3 output)¶

    Structure for the one-page draft:

    1. The finding: RobotArena∞ shows current VLAs have weak cross-dataset generalization and are highly sensitive to environmental perturbation.
    2. The mechanistic hypothesis: static-image visual representations are structurally insufficient for dynamic manipulation, either because action fine-tuning erodes semantic understanding (embodiment tax) or because per-frame representations never encoded the dynamics needed for control in the first place.
    3. Two candidate fixes from the literature: architectural separation of semantic and visuomotor pathways (UAM/TwinBrainVLA) vs. obtaining dynamics from video-based action models (VPP/VERA/Motus).
    4. One concrete experiment: extend RobotArena∞'s real-to-sim perturbation pipeline to compare an image-conditioned VLA, a dual-stream VLA, and a video-based action model under identical perturbation axes.

    Pick one thread for a deeper two-week pass:
