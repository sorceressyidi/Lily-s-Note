    4D Gaussian Splatting: Real-Time Photorealistic Dynamic Scene Representation¶

    Paper Overview

    Title: Real-Time Photorealistic Dynamic Scene Representation and Rendering with 4D Gaussian Splatting
    Authors: Zeyu Yang, Hongye Yang, Zijie Pan, et al.
    Venue: ICLR 2024
    Key Contribution: Extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to dynamic scenes by representing spatiotemporal data as 4D Gaussians, enabling real-time rendering of dynamic content.

    Problem Formulation¶

    Rendering Equation¶

    The image intensity at pixel \((u, v)\) and time \(t\) is computed as:

    \[\mathbb{I}(u, v, t) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} p_i(u, v, t) \cdot \alpha_i \cdot c_i(\mathbf{d}) \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - p_j(u, v, t) \cdot \alpha_j)\]

    Key Factorization

    The 4D Gaussian probability can be factorized as:

    \[p_i(u, v, t) = p_i(u, v | t) \cdot p_i(t)\]
    • Conditional 3D Gaussian: \(p_i(u, v | t)\) — spatial distribution at time \(t\)
    • Marginal 1D Gaussian: \(p_i(t)\) — temporal distribution

    4D Gaussian Representation¶

    Covariance Decomposition¶

    Similar to 3D Gaussian Splatting, the 4D covariance matrix is decomposed as:

    \[\Sigma = R S S^T R^T\]

    Where:

    Component Description Parameters
    \(S\) Scaling matrix \(\text{diag}(s_x, s_y, s_z, s_t)\)
    \(R\) Rotation matrix Two quaternions \(q_l, q_r\)
    \(\mu\) Mean position \((x, y, z, t)\)

    4D Rotation via Quaternions¶

    Isoclinic Decomposition

    A 4D rotation can be decomposed into two isoclinic rotations, each represented by a quaternion:

    \[R = L(q_l) \cdot R(q_r)\]

    Left Quaternion Matrix \(L(q_l)\) where \(q_l = (a, b, c, d)\):

    \[L(q_l) = \begin{pmatrix} a & -b & -c & -d \\ b & a & -d & c \\ c & d & a & -b \\ d & -c & b & a \end{pmatrix}\]

    Right Quaternion Matrix \(R(q_r)\) where \(q_r = (p, q, r, s)\):

    \[R(q_r) = \begin{pmatrix} p & -q & -r & -s \\ q & p & s & -r \\ r & -s & p & q \\ s & r & -q & p \end{pmatrix}\]

    Conditional and Marginal Gaussians¶

    Deriving the Conditional 3D Gaussian¶

    Given the 4D covariance partitioned as:

    \[\Sigma = \begin{pmatrix} \Sigma_{xyz} & \Sigma_{xyz,t} \\ \Sigma_{xyz,t}^T & \Sigma_t \end{pmatrix}\]

    The conditional 3D Gaussian (spatial distribution at time \(t\)):

    \[\Sigma_{xyz|t} = \Sigma_{xyz} - \Sigma_{xyz,t} \Sigma_t^{-1} \Sigma_{xyz,t}^T\]
    \[\mu_{xyz|t} = \mu_{xyz} + \Sigma_{xyz,t} \Sigma_t^{-1} (t - \mu_t)\]

    Marginal 1D Gaussian¶

    The temporal distribution is simply:

    \[p(t) = \mathcal{N}(t; \mu_t, \Sigma_t)\]

    4D Spherindrical Harmonics¶

    Background: Spherical Harmonics¶

    What are Spherical Harmonics?

    Orthogonal functions on the sphere surface, used to represent view-dependent appearance:

    \[f(\theta, \phi) \approx \sum_l \sum_{m=-l}^{l} c_l^m Y_l^m(\theta, \phi)\]

    Definition:

    \[Y_l^m(\theta, \phi) = \sqrt{\frac{(2l+1)(l-m)!}{4\pi(l+m)!}} P_l^m(\cos\theta) e^{im\phi}\]

    Where:

    • \(l\) — degree (non-negative integer)
    • \(m\) — order (\(-l \leq m \leq l\))
    • \(P_l^m\) — associated Legendre polynomials
    • \(c_l^m\) — SH coefficients

    Extension to 4D¶

    4D Spherindrical Harmonics

    To handle time-varying appearance, spherical harmonics are extended with a temporal component:

    \[Z_{nl}^m(t, \theta, \phi) = \cos\left(\frac{2\pi n t}{T}\right) Y_l^m(\theta, \phi)\]

    This allows modeling periodic color changes over time.

    Summary¶

    Aspect 3D-GS 4D-GS
    Scene type Static Dynamic
    Gaussian dimension 3D 4D (space + time)
    Rotation 1 quaternion 2 quaternions
    View-dependent color Spherical Harmonics Spherindrical Harmonics
    Real-time rendering ✅ ✅
